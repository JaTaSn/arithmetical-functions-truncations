# ternary — speculative

Nothing here yet, and unlike `unitary-revisited/` there is no prior paper to revisit: **no
truncation analysis of the ternary convolution exists.**

## The idea

Dirichlet convolution is the coarsest of Narkiewicz's regular convolutions; the unitary convolution
is another. The **ternary** convolution — all blocks of size 3, introduced jointly with Elin Gawell
in her 2005 bachelor's thesis — sits in the same lattice and has never had its truncations
described. The open question, long flagged in the author's notes, is:

> Redo the truncation-and-resolution analysis for the ternary convolution, or for the general
> greedy convolution of length d. Which truncation ideals are stable? Does an analogue of
> `C_{n,v} = Φ(n, p_v)` hold?

## Why it might work, and why it might not

In favour: the Dirichlet proof is short and its mechanism is transparent — it turns on writing every
`P`-rough integer uniquely as `Pᵃm′` and letting a difference of counting functions telescope. Any
convolution whose divisor structure supports that factorization is a candidate.

Against: the Dirichlet argument leans on the truncation ideal being generated by *all* monomials
above a weight, which is what makes it stable in the reversed variable order. There is no reason yet
to expect the ternary truncation ideals to be stable at all — and if they are not, the
Eliahou–Kervaire route is unavailable and something else is needed.

A computational first pass exists in the author's private notes (ternary [n]-truncations built for
n = 10, 15, 20, with socle-dimension data), which is the natural place to start.
