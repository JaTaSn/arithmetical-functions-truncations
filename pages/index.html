Arithmetical functions: truncations, revisited

Jan Snellman · Matematiska Institutionen, Linköpings Universitet

The ring Γ of all functions ℕ+ → K under Dirichlet convolution is an inverse limit of finite-dimensional truncations Γn. Each is a polynomial ring modulo a monomial ideal that is stable once the variables are reversed, so it has a computable minimal free resolution — the subject of a 2000 paper, revisited here.

The result

The generator counts governing that resolution turn out to be values of the classical sieve:

Cn,v = Φ(n, pv)

Legendre’s sifting function — the number of integers in [1, n] with no prime factor ≤ pv. From this follow a proof of the 2000 paper’s Conjecture 4.6, left open there; the average order Cn ~ π(n)²/2, showing that paper’s own lower bound is asymptotically an equality; the identification of Cn with OEIS A182843; and errata — one stated result of the 2000 paper is false, and two of its proofs are incomplete.

Reproducing everything

The repository carries every script needed to reproduce both papers’ numbers: SageMath (which recomputes the generator counts by direct enumeration, deliberately not via the formulas under test), the original 1999 Maple code, and a Macaulay2 check that confirms the 2000 paper’s Betti-number formula against genuine minimal free resolutions for n = 2…40.
